Section 01 — Scope & framing

How to read this document

This map is deliberately structured as a starting frame, not a closed proposal. It articulates a hypothesis — based on what we heard in our session and what we know about comparable post-carve-out finance functions — that we expect to refine in the joint workshop with the Aptia team.

Scope. This use-case map covers Aptia's corporate finance function — the ~70-person team that runs the financial operations of Aptia Group as a corporate entity (billing clients, paying suppliers, closing books, FP&A, tax, treasury, controls).

It does not cover Aptia's pensions administration operations — member calculations, GMP equalisation, benefit processing, scheme communications, dashboards compliance, regulatory reporting to TPR / DWP / FCA. That work sits under operations leadership and forms a separate transformation conversation.

Anchored on what was said in the session

The session articulated a target ambition of running a ~3,000-person organisation with a finance function of ~20 people — conditional on data adequacy, clean processes, proper documentation, and effective AI usage. The current finance function is ~70 heads, equating to ~2% of Aptia's ~3,500 global employees.

The three-layer framework

We have organised every use case in this document against three layers — Intelligence, Execution, and Governance & Control — and applied a consistent set of attributes to each: layer placement, AI-led vs AI-assisted split, human touchpoint, plug-and-play vs configurable vs bespoke, and ROI evidence (where comparable references exist).

Section 06 — Architecture posture

How we design — fitted to your stack and your direction

Knowing the systems estate matters as much as knowing the use cases. We have designed against what Aptia is using today and where the infrastructure is going.

D365 — finance backbone

Every Execution-layer use case is designed to operate inside or alongside D365 via Power Platform connectors and direct APIs. We do not propose to replace it; we propose to make it more productive.

BlackLine — conditional

If BlackLine is deployed for reconciliations, journals or close management, our R2R agents layer on top of it via API — handling the exception-heavy 20% that BlackLine flags but still requires human investigation. If BlackLine is not deployed, AWS-native equivalents do the same job.

Workday Adaptive — FP&A engine

Phase 3 Intelligence-layer use cases — variance commentary, forecasting, board pack assembly — are built on top of Adaptive's data via API. The platform stays as the source of truth.

Simples + D365 — the time-to-bill spine

Recently rolled out, Simples now feeds D365 for the services-billing pipeline. Our O2C agents are designed to operate across the full Simples → D365 → invoice → cash chain, not as point fixes inside D365 alone.

AWS-native by default

Aligned to your stated direction — agents run on Bedrock for LLM orchestration, Lambda for execution, S3 for stateful storage.

One partner, no patchwork

Every use case in this document is designed to operate as part of one integrated finance AI estate, owned by a single pod, not a portfolio of niche vendors. This directly addresses the fragmentation concern raised in the session.

Section 07 — Concerns we heard, addressed

Three concerns from the session, three answers

You raised three substantive concerns about AI in finance generally and vendor relationships specifically. We address them here directly rather than leaving them to be uncovered later.

A

Fewer, deeper agents — not 300 of them

Our entire bridge totals roughly 22 use cases across three phases — not 300 agents. Each one is sized to address a specific FTE pool, with a measurable outcome. Bot-sprawl is the failure mode we are designing against, not the model we are proposing.

"Example cited where AP still had 300–400 agents, delivering limited incremental value." — Session note, 30 April.
B

Your IP stays yours

Code, configurations, and deployment artefacts sit inside Aptia's environment from day one. Our accelerators are scaffolding, not lock-in — they speed delivery but do not constrain ownership. Standard knowledge-transfer language at the end of any engagement; your team can run what we build.

"Concern that Aptia could be helping build IP transferable to competitors." — Session note.
C

One partner, one pod, no patchwork

This document deliberately presents a single integrated estate of use cases — not a vendor menu. One pod owns the full Phase 2 + Phase 3 outcome. No handoff risk between vendors.

"Preference for holistic finance solution. Avoid patchwork of multiple niche vendors." — Session note.
Section 08 — Featured agentic stories

Three engagements that show what agentic finance looks like in practice

A full set of comparable engagements is embedded directly in the use-case matrix above — click any populated cell to see the corresponding agentic story (problem, agent design, AI stack, outcomes, and the Aptia parallel). Below are three of the strongest references, called out for emphasis.

O2C — $30M unlock
Multinational Chemical Manufacturer
The agentic workflow

Agents read SAP transaction data across regions, customer payment behaviour, and dispute history; decided credit-risk profile, dunning priority, and dispute categorisation; triggered automated dunning, credit-limit alerts, and exception routing; coordinated across regional collections, central finance, and a newly-instituted Global Process Owner.

Outcomes that matter

DSO 53 → 42 days · Past-due AR 8% → 4% · $30M working capital unlocked · Investment recovered within Year 1.

FP&A — Bain-equivalent reporting
Leading Private Equity Firm — portfolio MIS
The agentic workflow

Agents read across portfolio company source systems (sales, operations, expense, finance, audit) by logging in directly; decided data validity using checklists and business rules; triggered storage to a centralised reporting data warehouse and onward feed to BI tools and third-party portfolio platforms (IHS Markit, ION); coordinated end-to-end straight-through reporting from collection to distribution.

Outcomes that matter

80% reduction in data-collection effort · 70% reduction in reporting errors · 80% improvement in SLA adherence for reporting deadlines · Straight-through reporting enabled.

Controls — GenAI compliance
Large European Bank — Fetch policy search
The agentic workflow

Agents read across the bank's policy library; decided semantic relevance to user queries with multi-lingual support; triggered structured response generation with citations and document comparison; coordinated within the user's authorised access boundary, ensuring users only saw documents they were permitted to view.

Outcomes that matter

99% reduction in query resolution time · Multi-region / multi-lingual capability · Low running cost via serverless infrastructure · Pinpoint-accurate fine-grained responses.
